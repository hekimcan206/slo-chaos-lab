# SLOs for the ledger posting endpoint

Service: `ledger-api`. User journey: a client posts a journal entry (`POST /api/v1/journal-entries`) and expects it to
be recorded exactly once, quickly. Period: 30 days, request-based. Source of truth: `src/SloChaosLab.Slo/SloCatalog.cs`;
the rules in `deploy/prometheus/rules/slo-rules.yml` are generated from it (ADR 0003).

| SLO | SLI (good events / eligible events) | Objective | Error budget | At 50 req/s, budget per 30 days | Alerted |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `ledger-post-availability` | posts not answered with 5xx / all posts | 99.9 % | 0.1 % | 129,600 failed posts (43 min of total outage) | yes |
| `ledger-post-latency-p50` | non-5xx posts within 25 ms / non-5xx posts | 50 % | 50 % | - | no: 14.4 x 50 % > 100 %, unreachable |
| `ledger-post-latency-p95` | non-5xx posts within 100 ms / non-5xx posts | 95 % | 5 % | 6.48 M slow posts | yes |
| `ledger-post-latency-p99` | non-5xx posts within 250 ms / non-5xx posts | 99 % | 1 % | 1.296 M slow posts | yes |

Why these thresholds: the measured baseline at 50 req/s is p50 4-5 ms, p95 8-11 ms, p99 16-17 ms (medians of three windows) (see
[benchmarks](benchmarks.md)), so all three latency objectives hold with wide margin on a healthy system. They are set
for a user-facing write path that sits behind another service with a 2 s timeout, not tightened to the lab's numbers.

## Alerts (Google SRE Workbook, chapter 5)

| Alert suffix | Long window | Short window | Burn rate | Budget spent when it fires | Severity |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `FastBurn` | 1 h | 5 m | 14.4 | 2 % | page |
| `SlowBurn` | 6 h | 30 m | 6 | 5 % | page |
| `BudgetErosion` | 3 d | 6 h | 1 | 10 % | ticket |

Full names: `LedgerPostAvailabilityFastBurn`, `LedgerPostLatencyP99SlowBurn`, and so on. An alert fires only when
both windows exceed the burn rate. Recording rules: `slo:sli_error:ratio_rate<window>`, `slo:burn_rate:rate<window>`,
`slo:error_budget_remaining:ratio`, `slo:objective:ratio`, plus `slo:latency_seconds:p{50,95,99}_rate1m` for charts.

## Dashboard

Grafana, "Ledger posting SLOs" (http://localhost:58300, provisioned read-only, generated by
`tools/dashboard/build-dashboard.mjs`): 5-minute SLIs, budget remaining, firing alerts, requests by status, latency
quantiles against the 250 ms line, availability and p99 burn rates against the 6x and 14.4x lines, an alert timeline,
the request-path connection pool, bytes on the database link, and retry/breaker outcomes. Traces and structured logs
for individual requests are in the Aspire dashboard (http://localhost:58388).

## Runbook

**`LedgerPostAvailability*Burn`: posts are failing with 5xx.**
1. Grafana: is it all requests (link or database down) or a fraction (resets, overload)? Check "Requests per second
   by status" and the posting outcomes panel: `short_circuited` means the breaker is open, `shed` means the
   concurrency limit is rejecting, `failed` means attempts are timing out or erroring.
2. `GET /health/ready` on the service: unhealthy means the database is unreachable from the service.
3. Aspire dashboard: open a failed trace; the Npgsql span shows whether connect, a command or a timeout failed.
4. In the lab: `curl -s localhost:58474/proxies/postgres/toxics` shows active faults; `curl -X POST localhost:58474/reset`
   removes them.
5. Callers should retry with the same Idempotency-Key; the service guarantees a retry never posts twice.

**`LedgerPostLatencyP99*Burn` / `P95*Burn`: posts succeed but slowly.**
1. Compare the latency quantiles with "Bytes to and from PostgreSQL" and the pool panel: latency on the link shows as
   higher p50 with flat throughput; bandwidth shows as queued connections (pending requests) and pool growth.
2. Check the posting mode at `GET /api/v1/lab`: `Interactive` pays link latency four times per post (ADR 0005).
3. A latency burn with *no* availability burn on a partitioned database means callers are giving up (499); see
   postmortem 5. In the hardened profile this case surfaces as 503s instead.

**Budget policy (lab):** if a 30-day budget is exhausted, reliability work takes priority over features until it
recovers, as in the SRE Workbook's example error budget policy.
